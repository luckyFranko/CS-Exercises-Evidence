The enrollment helper is assumed to store student personal data, which means that basic GDPR responsibilities are relevant. Personal data should only be collected and processed for a clear and legitimate purpose related to the operation of the enrollment system. Access to this data should be limited to authorized personnel and appropriate security measures should be used to protect it from unauthorized access. During this seminar, the database password was stored in plain text inside secrets.env, which demonstrated a potential security weakness. If LabOps discovered that the database password had been exposed, the first step would be to change the password immediately and investigate whether any unauthorized access heppened. Depending on the circumstances, LabOps might also need to assess whether the incident qualifies as a personal data breach and whether affected individuals or relevant authorities should be notified. This report is not a legal opinion but a general discussion of security responsibilities.

Based on the information provided in the scenario, LabOps would most likely not be considered an essential entity under NIS2. The organization is described as a small team maintaining an internal enrollment helper rather than operating critical national infrastructure or providing an essential service at a large scale. It could potentially be considered an important entity only if it were part of a larger educational institution that falls within the scope of NIS2. However, the available information is insufficient to make a definitive determination. Regardless of its regulatory status, LabOps should still apply good cybersecurity practices because the system processes personal data and provides access to important services.

All practical activities performed for this seminar were limited to the local Docker container, locally created files, and a phishing demonstration page hosted on the student's own computer. No scans, attacks, or tests were directed at external systems, networks, or real users. The purpose of the exercises was solely educational and all actions took place within the controlled laboratory environment. Before testing any third-party server or system, explicit written permission from the owner would be required. Without proper authorization, such testing could be unethical and may violate organizational policies or applicable laws.
